Site Payments Platform

Our team is replacing a VB6 application that has calculated clinical-trial site payments for over 20 years with a modern platform — one place to model site contracts, price every payable event and pay the right institution, whether the data comes from DataFax, TrialMaster or REDCap.

Status
In progress
Context
Hamilton Health Sciences · PHRI
Role
Developer, modernization team
Stack
C# / .NET · SQL Server

Why we're rebuilding it

Clinical trials pay the hospitals and investigators who run them — usually per participant visit, as written in each site's contract. For more than 20 years, our site payments were calculated by a VB6 desktop application, supported by spreadsheets and SQL that only a few people understood.

Each data source also had its own way of building payment reports. Studies captured in DataFax, TrialMaster and REDCap each needed separate logic, so changing a contract, adding a study or answering "what exactly did we agree to pay last year?" was slow and risky.

Our team is replacing it with a single web platform: model each site contract once, keep every version of it, and calculate payments the same way no matter which system the clinical data comes from.

The contract says: for every completed 6-month visit at this site, pay these people these amounts. We enter that table once — and keep every version of it.

Names and amounts on this page are illustrative, not a real agreement.

How a payable item reaches a party

The new domain model answers five questions in order. Each one is its own entity with its own rules, which is what lets the platform stay independent of any single data source.

  1. What?Payable Item

    Something the study pays for, such as a completed 6-month visit.

  2. When earned?Payment Criteria

    The rule that says a participant has earned that item, evaluated against EDC data.

  3. Who?Payable Party

    A person or office the study pays: investigator, coordinator, hospital, national office.

  4. How much, where?Fee Schedule

    The contract's fee appendix: amounts per item and party for a set of sites, in one country and currency.

  5. Paid to whom?Vendor

    The approved institution that actually receives the money — closer to a bank account than a person.

Payable items and parties belong to a study. Fee schedules belong to a study and cover a set of centre codes. Vendors sit above every study, at the organization level — one hospital can be paid for many roles across many trials.

One row, followed all the way through

Participant 101-0007 completes the 6-month follow-up. The payment criteria say that item is earned. Centre 101 is covered by one fee schedule, whose 6-month row splits the fee between three parties — and all three map to the same hospital vendor, so finance sends a single payment.

What makes it more than a data pipeline

The interesting engineering is in turning contract rules into a model that can't drift out of sync with reality.

  • Contracts as versioned data

    Fee schedules move from Draft to Published with semantic versions (v1.0.0 → v1.1.0 → v2.0.0). A change branches a single revision draft; publishing replaces the original, so there is always exactly one working version.

  • Point-in-time history

    Open any published version and see the whole contract exactly as it was — years later. It replaces digging through a field-by-field audit log.

  • Invariants enforced by the domain

    Every centre code belongs to exactly one fee schedule; a centre contract covers exactly one site; a party's country must match the contract; amounts can't be negative; items in use can't be deleted; parties are archived, never deleted.

  • Inputs people actually type

    Centre scopes accept lists and overlapping ranges — 1,2,5-7,7-9 — are stored code by code, and echoed back normalized as 1, 2, 5-9 so typos are caught immediately.

  • People change, contracts don't

    Replace Party swaps a departing coordinator for their successor on a revision and keeps every amount, then publishes it as a minor version with a dated description.

  • Pay institutions, not people

    An organization-level vendor registry, aligned with the hospital's finance vendor rules. Parties from every study wait in a Pending queue and are mapped to an active vendor in the same country — deliberately a human step.

  • Source-agnostic by design

    Payment criteria are defined against a common model, so the same fee schedule logic can be fed from DataFax, TrialMaster or REDCap instead of one bespoke report per system.

  • Guard-railed editing

    Unsaved allocation changes block structural actions (add, remove, replace, publish), published schedules are read-only, and permissions are split by role — editing, archiving, party management and vendor management.

Roadmap

The configuration layer is in place; the parts that consume it are next.

  1. In progressPayment run engine

    Take completed visits, find the fee schedule that applies to each site, and produce payable amounts — reproducibly, against the exact contract version in force.

  2. NextCriteria reports

    Link each payable item to the source report that proves it was earned, across DataFax, TrialMaster and REDCap.

  3. PlannedPayment status & vendor totals

    Paid / pending status per participant, and running totals per vendor across studies to flag when an institution crosses the fiscal-audit threshold.
